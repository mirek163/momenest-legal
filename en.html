Privacy policy

In effect from September 24, 2026

What data Momenest holds about you, why, where it lives, who sees it, for how long, and what you can do with it. In short: we do not sell it, do not use it for advertising and do not track what you do outside the app.

Who is responsible for the data

The operator of Momenest is responsible for your data: Miroslav Baša. You can reach them straight from the app (Settings → Help and feedback) or by e-mail at momenest.support@gmail.com.

Without an account, everything stays on the phone

Without an account nothing leaves the phone. Chores, rewards, the garden, the settings — all of it is stored on this phone only. Something goes out only when you do it yourself: you write to support, turn on error reporting, use the barcode scanner or add something to Google Calendar. Each is described below. Uninstalling the app deletes the data on the phone.

What we store when you have an account

With an account you get backup, groups and friends — and for that we store on the server:

  • account details: your e-mail address and account identifier; a password only in unreadable (hashed) form. When you sign in with Google, Google also passes on your name and a link to the profile photo of your Google account — the app does not use them, but the sign-in service stores them;
  • your profile: a name or nickname, an avatar and a profile photo if you upload one. The photo is made smaller on the phone first, and its metadata, location included, is removed;
  • what you create in the app: chores and their notes, completions and skips, rewards and wishes, routines, events, things at home (for example the purchase date and the end of the warranty), shopping lists with their items, prices and trips, days away (dates only, no reason) and the garden;
  • game progress: experience, coins, streaks, achievements and the group’s activity history;
  • the app’s settings;
  • friends, friend requests, blocked people and invitations to groups;
  • when you last finished a chore — one date and time, without which chore or where — and the nudges between you and your friends: who, whom, when and how many days without a finished chore;
  • when you accepted the terms of use, and which version.

Drinking log — health data

What you drink is seen by you alone — nobody in the group, not even its administrator. When you turn the hydration log on, Momenest stores what and how much you drank, your daily target and, if you have one, a drinking instruction from your doctor. This is health data, so we process it only with your explicit consent — you give it by turning the feature on yourself and entering the data yourself. You can withdraw it at any time: turn the log off and delete the entries — one by one in the drink overview, all at once by deleting the account or on request. The hydration log is for adults; a group member with the role Child does not see it.

Who can see your data

Your data is seen only by the people you share something with:

  • Members of a group see what belongs to the group: chores, who finished what, rewards, wishes, events, things at home, shopping lists, the activity history, and your name, avatar and profile photo. A private shopping list is seen only by you.
  • Nobody else sees your own space.
  • Friends, and anyone you send a friend request to, see your name and avatar, not your photo. Friends also see when you last finished a chore and can nudge you once a day — until you switch that off in the friends list; off means they see nothing and cannot nudge. Only somebody who knows your friend code can find you, and somebody you have blocked cannot find you at all.
  • Nobody but you sees the drinking log.
  • The operator accesses data on the server only when that is needed to run the service, fix an error, handle your request or a report, or when the law requires it.

We do not sell, rent or pass data to anyone else for advertising.

Support, feedback and reports

When you write to us, we keep only what we need to answer: the message, a reply e-mail if you fill one in, a screenshot if you attach one, and technical details — the app version, the system and its version, the phone model, the language, the appearance and the part of the app you are writing from. Diagnostics are attached only if you tick them yourself; they hold error codes and kinds, never the content of chores, names or e-mails. Without an account the message is tied only to a random installation code, in a form the code cannot be recovered from — it serves only to protect against abuse.

When you report a person or content, we store the report and the account it concerns. A person reads every report.

Error reports

Error reporting is off until you turn it on yourself in Settings. When it is on, the app sends the Sentry service, on an error, the kind of error, the place in the code, the name of the screen (not what was on it), the app version and whether you were signed in. Never a chore's title, a note, a name, an e-mail, an account identifier, or what you did and when. It can be turned off at any time, and that takes effect at once.

Scanner, Google Calendar, notifications and updates

Four things reach outside the app — and here is exactly what leaves with each.

The barcode scanner uses the camera only while you have it open. The code is recognised by the Google ML Kit library on the phone itself; the camera image is neither sent anywhere nor stored, and the code read is saved with the product on the shopping list. The ML Kit library does, however, send Google technical data — about the device and the app, an installation identifier, response times and error codes — for diagnostics and usage statistics.

When you choose to add an event or a chore to Google Calendar, your calendar opens with the title, date and note filled in. Google receives that data the moment the calendar opens, and only you save it; from then on Google's policies apply to it.

When you have the app from Google Play, it asks the Play Store after it starts whether a newer version is available. To answer, the Play Store sends Google information about the device, the app’s version and its installed parts; according to Google it is encrypted, passed on to nobody and deleted after a fixed period. Momenest adds nothing about you to that question and keeps the answer nowhere.

Reminders and notifications are created by the app on the phone itself. No server sends them to you.

Why we process data

Every purpose has a legal basis:

  • The account, backup, groups, friends, support and everything the app does when you use it: performance of a contract, that is the terms of use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • The drinking log: your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR).
  • Error reports: your consent, given by turning them on in Settings (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).
  • Security and protection against abuse — limits on the number of messages, blocking, reports, server logs — and the ML Kit library’s technical data: a legitimate interest in a secure, working service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Who processes the data, and where

Three services without which an account would not work — all of them acting only on our instructions:

  • Supabase (Supabase, Inc.) — the database, sign-in and file storage. The data is kept in a data centre in Ireland, in the EU. Supabase also sends the e-mails that confirm an account and change a password.
  • Google — sign-in with a Google account, the ML Kit library in the scanner, and the Google Play Store, which answers the question about a new version.
  • Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.) — error reports, and only when you turn them on.

Supabase, Google and Sentry are American companies or belong to one. Where data leaves the EU, this happens under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission.

How long we keep data

We keep data for as long as it is of use — and then delete it:

  • The account and everything that belongs to it, until you delete the account.
  • When you leave a group, what you did in it stays in its history, with your name — for as long as the group exists or until you delete your account.
  • Support requests and reports, for as long as we need them to handle them and to protect others from repeated breaches of the rules, but no longer than 2 years after they are resolved. Your own requests are deleted with your account.
  • The server’s connection logs (for example the IP address and time) are deleted automatically within a few days.
  • You delete the data on the phone by uninstalling the app.

Deleting your account

You delete the account yourself, right in the app: Settings → Delete account. If you no longer have the app, write to momenest.support@gmail.com from the address the account is tied to, and we will delete the account within 30 days at the latest.

Deleting the account removes the sign-in details, the profile and the profile picture, your memberships in groups and everything tied to you — completions, coins, experience, wishes, days away, private shopping lists, the activity history by you and about you, and the hydration entries — as well as friends and blocks, the record of your agreement to the terms, and your support requests including screenshots. Groups you founded disappear entirely, for the other members too.

What remains is only what belongs to somebody else's group: the chores, routines, events, household items and shopping items you added to it — without your name. And reports somebody else made about you, without a link to your account.

Your rights

The data is yours, so you can: ask for access to it and for a copy, have it corrected or erased, restrict its processing, take it with you (portability) and object to processing based on legitimate interest. You can withdraw consent at any time; what was processed before does not become unlawful. Just write straight from the app (Settings → Help and feedback) or by e-mail at momenest.support@gmail.com — we answer within one month at the latest. If you believe we handle your data unlawfully, you can complain to the Czech Office for Personal Data Protection (www.uoou.gov.cz).

Children

Children can use Momenest too. A child can open an account of their own from the age of 15; a younger child only with the consent of a parent or another legal guardian. A parent can ask at any time for access to their child's data or for the child's account to be deleted. If we learn that an account of a child under 15 was created without a parent's consent, we delete it. In a group the administrator can give a member the role Child; such a member does not see features meant for adults, such as the hydration log.

Security

The connection to the server is encrypted, and the sign-in lives in the phone's secure storage. On the server everybody reaches only their own data and their groups' data — rules in the database itself enforce it, not just the app. Profile pictures and screenshots are kept in private storage.

Changes to this policy

When the policy changes, you will see a new version with a new date in the app; a significant change is announced in What's new.
